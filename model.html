Think of every question you could possibly be curious about as pointing in a different direction from where you stand. Animals that way. Car engines that way. Why your friend went quiet at lunch, that way. There are infinite directions, so nobody covers them all.

Each line in the drawing is one question you actually hold. Which way it points is what the question is about. How long it is, is how far you'll chase it. A short line means you'd nod if someone brought it up. A long line means you'd stay up reading about it.

The dashed circle near the middle is the price of admission. A question has to get past it before you do anything about it. Grey dashed lines never made it — those are the things you find kind of interesting and never once looked up.

The outer shape is not a ball you live inside. It's the shape your questions make. Go deep on one thing and it stretches out over there like a tent pole. Stop caring about something and a dent appears.

The number in the corner is a made-up score, not a measurement. It adds up how far you're chasing things and puts it on a ten-point scale so you can watch it move. Treat it like a thermometer someone built in their garage: useful for spotting change, not for proving anything.

The gold band is your ring. It's not drawn by hand — it's worked out from where your live questions actually sit. It's the answer to "which directions can curiosity still get in?" Wide band, lots of ways in. Thin band, only a few.

Infinite directions exist. Only your reach is drawn.

Build it on purpose

Curiosity you decide to have, rather than curiosity you happen to have.

It plants in your biggest dent — the direction you're most closed off in. Push effort up and watch what happens to the directions next door.

Why it fills more than one direction

You can't decide to be curious about everything. You can decide to be curious about one thing.

Chase that one thing far enough and the questions beside it come along free — learn about birds and you end up wondering about dinosaurs, migration, why bones are hollow. That's the purple patch spreading.

Watch the score in the corner of the drawing. That number is the argument.
