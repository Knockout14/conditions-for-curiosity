Conditions for Curiosity  ·  a podcast in progress

Curiosity is not a personality trait. It is what a room makes possible.

Twenty years in classrooms taught me that wondering shows up when the conditions are right and vanishes when they aren't. This show is about building those conditions on purpose.

The argument

  • People don't simply have curiosity.
  • Curiosity emerges when the conditions are right.
  • Those conditions can be designed.

Psychologists have been measuring curiosity for fifty years. Nearly all of that work treats it as a trait. Something you score high or low on. Something sitting inside the person.

That research isn't wrong. It's answering a different question than the one I have.

My question is the one you have at 8:40 on a Tuesday. Not which of these kids is curious. What would make curiosity likely in this room, with these thirty, today. Almost nobody publishes on that. The people who know it are teaching fifth period. The people writing the papers aren't standing there when it falls apart.

I taught middle and high school math. I coached teachers for ten years. I led curriculum at a national level. I finished a master's in educational research to get closer to the problem.

I'll talk about what I've seen and what some research points to. I'll identify where those agree and where I am still working it out. You will be able to tell which is which.

The Curiosity Sphere

Every question you could be curious about points in a direction. How far you'll chase it is how long the line runs. Go deep on one thing and the shape stretches out over there like a tent pole. Quit caring about something and a dent shows up. What's left is your ring: the directions curiosity can still get in.

I made it because I couldn't tell whether the idea held up until I tried to make it hold still. It's a working model, not a finding. Drag it around, and tell me where it stops making sense.
