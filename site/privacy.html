Privacy

What I collect, and why.

Short version: I collect what's needed to run the podcast newsletter and the companion app, I don't sell or share it, and I've deliberately kept the app from ever asking for your child's exact age, birthdate, or name.

Last updated: September 25, 2026.

Who this is

Conditions for Curiosity is a one-person project — I'm Kevin, I host the podcast, and I built the companion app myself. There's no company, no team with access to this data, and no third party I sell it to. If you have a question about any of this, email me directly: conditionsforcuriosity@gmail.com.

The podcast site

If you sign up for the newsletter, I collect your email address — nothing else. That signup is handled by Buttondown, a third-party email service; your address is stored on their servers, not mine, and their own privacy policy governs how they handle it. Right now that's a weekly note — something I'm reading about curiosity in kids, one thing to try that night, and a standing ask for how the app's working for you. It'll change shape again once season two is scripted. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe.

The Curiosity Sphere model (/model.html) collects nothing at all — it runs entirely in your browser and doesn't talk to any server.

This site can use Google Analytics to see which pages and episodes people are actually finding — standard, aggregate stuff: page views, roughly where visitors come from, what kind of device they're on. Nothing about you individually. It only runs if you click Accept on the banner the first time you visit; click Decline (or just ignore it) and the Analytics script never loads at all — not quietly in the background, not at all. You can change your answer any time from the "Cookie preferences" link in this footer. Google's own privacy policy governs how they handle what Analytics collects. This only runs on the podcast site — the app (everything under /app) has no analytics of any kind, on top of everything above.

The app

The companion app is where most of the actual data collection happens, because it's built around real feedback from real families using it. Here's exactly what it collects and why:

  • Your first name — so I don't mix your feedback up with another family's.
  • Your email, optional — only so I can follow up if something you share is worth asking about. Leave it blank and I have no way to reach you.
  • Your child's age band (3–4, 5–6, or 7–8+) — never an exact age or birthdate. This only shapes which questions the app offers.
  • Your answers to a short comprehension check during onboarding, and which three questions you pick each week.
  • Circle-back responses — whether your child asked a question back, and an optional note, written by you, on what they said.
  • A random session ID, generated by your device, used to tell your submissions apart from another family's — it isn't tied to any account or login, because there isn't one.

This is sent to a private spreadsheet only I can read, through a small backend function — it doesn't pass through any other company. Your day-to-day progress in the app (which screen you're on, which questions you've already been asked) is saved in your own browser's local storage, on your own device — I never see that part at all unless it's the specific data listed above, submitted when you actually complete a step.

I read this weekly, by hand, to see how the app is actually landing with real families. I don't run ads against it and I don't have a use for your data beyond that — there's nothing else here to monetize it with.

Counting games (pilot)

The counting games are a separate pilot, at their own web address, for a small group of families I've invited. They collect:

  • Your first name and, optionally, your email — same as the main app, and for the same reasons.
  • Your child's age band — never an exact age or birthdate.
  • A label for each child that you choose — initials, "older" or "younger," anything but their name. It only keeps two kids' nights from getting mixed up.
  • What happened in the game: your answers to a few short multiple-choice questions, and what you choose to share in your own words (typed, or dictated with your phone's keyboard mic — only the text is saved, never a recording).
  • How long the short questions took you, so I can spot entries that were rushed or accidental.

This goes to the same private spreadsheet as the main app. When you share at least a couple of sentences, I write a short note back to you myself, and it appears in the app the next time you open it.

How AI is and isn't involved. To help me write back, an AI model may read your share and draft ideas for me. That model runs on my own computer. Your words are not sent to any AI company, and nothing you share is used to train any AI. I rewrite or throw away every draft, and no note you receive is written by AI. If you'd rather no model read your words at all, tick the box when you start, or email me, and I'll write every note from scratch.

About your child's information

The app is built and used by you, the parent or caregiver — your child never opens it, creates anything in it, or submits anything to it directly. It is not directed at children, and I don't knowingly collect information directly from a child.

What I do collect, about your child, from you: an age band (never an exact age, birthdate, or name), whatever you choose to write in an optional circle-back note, and, in the counting games, a label you choose for each child and what you share about how the game went. That's a deliberate design choice, made before any of this was built — the app can do everything it needs to (match question difficulty, show you patterns over time) without ever turning your child into an identifiable record on my end.

What I don't do

  • I don't sell your data, or your child's information, to anyone.
  • I don't share it with advertisers or data brokers.
  • The only analytics on the podcast site is Google Analytics, and it only runs if you click Accept on the banner. Neither app has analytics of any kind, and there are no ad pixels or third-party trackers anywhere.
  • I don't send what you share to any AI company, and I don't use it to train any AI.

Your choices

Email conditionsforcuriosity@gmail.com any time to ask what I have on file for your family, have it corrected, or have it deleted. For the app specifically, clearing your browser's local storage removes your on-device progress immediately; email me to have your submitted rows removed from the spreadsheet too. For the newsletter, every email has an unsubscribe link that removes you from Buttondown directly.

Changes to this policy

If what I collect or how I use it changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top. For anything material — a new kind of data, a new place it goes — I'll say so plainly here rather than quietly editing the wording.
