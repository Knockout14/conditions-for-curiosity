## 1. Ownership: same show, decoupled build

**Decision: v1 ships under Conditions for Curiosity — same repo, `/app` path, same Netlify site — but built so it can be extracted to its own repo later without a rewrite.**

Reasoning:

- This isn't the "convenience" failure mode the Aug 31 site log warned against. The positioning memo (Thread 14) makes a specific, non-cosmetic argument: the podcast is the mechanism that installs the "learn from your child" frame *before* any content arrives, and every study in the memo's Thread 2 says the adult's frame at first contact determines whether what follows helps or hurts. Coupling the app to the show isn't reusing a pipeline — it's sequencing the actual intervention correctly.
- Season 1 just closed (Ep 8, Sept 1) with an audience that has already absorbed 8 episodes of framing, plus an active Buttondown list. That's a warm, frame-primed group to learn from — better than a cold audience on an independent domain.
- The honest counter-risk, which is why this isn't a clean call: if the strategic path later becomes Route B (academic partner) or Route C (institutional/district), a product page written in one host's voice, on a personal-brand domain, may read as less credible to an IRB or a district buyer than something neutral. That risk is real but deferrable — it's a re-skinning problem, not a re-architecting problem, *if* we build for it now.
- Concretely: app code, question-bank data, and circle-back data live in their own directory with their own data files (JSON, not baked into HTML strings), reusing the navy/gold theme as a default rather than a hard dependency. If Route B/C becomes real, extraction is a copy and a re-skin, not a rebuild.

## 2. Device, accounts, and infrastructure

- **Device: mobile web, phone-first.** Matches the actual use case (kitchen table, end of day) — not desktop-first, not a native app.
- **No accounts, no passwords.** Nothing in the core loop needs cross-device sync or a shared household login — it's always one adult, one phone, one kid, physically present.
- **Continuity rests on the device, not on identity.** Each device generates a random session identifier on first use; that's the actual source of truth that "this is the same family returning," not anything the family types in.
- **First name is a label, not a credential.** Captured so families can be told apart in the digest, with a soft uniqueness nudge ("that name's in use this week, add a last initial?") — a display convenience, not a security boundary.
- **Optional email, captured at onboarding.** Framed honestly ("so we can follow up if something in your feedback is worth asking about"). This is the reachability channel for anyone beyond the founder's personal network — the warm test group won't need it (he has their numbers), but it becomes load-bearing once the app opens to podcast listeners and friends-of-friends he can't just call. Treat email (when given) as the real disambiguator; first name stays a friendly label only.
- **Child's age is captured as a band (3–4 / 5–6 / 7–8), never an exact age or birthdate, and the child's name is never captured at all.** The band gives the full functional benefit (matching question difficulty, interpreting feedback in age-appropriate terms) without turning a coarse fact into an identifiable record about a specific child.
- **Backend: lightweight, not a real service.** Already on Netlify with serverless functions available (confirmed in the Aug 31 site log, correcting an earlier wrong assumption that Netlify was static-only). A function receiving circle-back submissions, tagged with the device's session ID, first name, optional email, and age band, writing to something as simple as a hosted spreadsheet or a small database, is enough — no user tables, no auth service.
- **Audience trajectory:** starts as a small warm test group (people the founder knows personally), then opens to podcast listeners / friends-of-friends — a degree removed, no direct line to call them. The email capture above exists specifically for that second phase.

## 3. Core loop — v1 scope

The loop has four stages: a one-time **onboarding**, a one-time-per-week **pick**, then the **move** and **circle-back** repeated across the week's three questions.

### 3a. Onboarding (once, first use only)

Four screens, never repeated after first pass, no per-session re-gating:

- **Episode-5 confirmation.** Not "have you listened to the podcast" generically — specifically "have you gotten to episode 5," because that's the episode the site itself calls the hinge ("everything after it assumes you've been through it"). Self-report, one tap, with a link out to the episode for anyone who taps "not yet."
- **Name, email, and age-band capture.** See §2 above.
- **A three-question comprehension check, soft-gated, targeted at the three controllable variables — not at the app's own mechanics.** The original check tested the reciprocal-disclosure mechanics (who answers first, how to handle a wrong answer), but those are already enforced structurally by the screens themselves — testing for them was redundant. What the app *can't* structurally guarantee is the actual thesis: the room, the adult's own curiosity, and the adult noticing curiosity in the child — the three variables named as the most controllable conditions for curiosity. One question per variable:
  - **The room** — which of these can you set up before your kid even walks in? (correct: something left out to notice, not their mood)
  - **You, in** — what matters more for whether your kid stays curious: whether you're actually curious too, or whether you already know the answer? (correct: your own curiosity — Engel's modeling finding)
  - **You, out** — your kid stops and stares mid-task; is that stalling or is that curiosity starting? (correct: noticing it as curiosity — the "notice the serve" step)

  Feedback is immediate and non-punitive either way, and **the continue button stays enabled regardless of the answers given** — a hard block here would turn the front door into the intrusive, evaluating posture the product is designed to avoid. The check exists to catch "didn't work because I had no idea what I was doing," not to gate entry on a passing score.

### 3b. The weekly pick (once a week)

The adult sees three candidate questions — pulled from Raw/Found stock, mixing General and Math domains on purpose (the "protected remainder" rule in §4 applies here directly) — and ranks them first, second, third for the week. Chosen once, consumed automatically one per session across the week; nothing to choose on any individual night. Editable anytime, not locked.

This balances a real tension: letting the adult opt out of questions they'd answer badly is a genuine improvement, but re-offering that choice every night would reintroduce a decision at exactly the moment — tired, end of day — every dosage precedent in the positioning memo (Beilock, Berkowitz, READY4K) says to protect.

### 3c. The move
- One reciprocal-disclosure question per session, adult answers too, pulled in ranked order from the week's picked three.
- Answer/seed is a post-child-answer object — never shown before or alongside the question (Callanan's Goldilocks-moment finding; positioning memo §9).
- The adult's own answer is typed into the app **before** turning to the child — a commitment device, not a script to read aloud.
- v1 pulls only from **Raw** and **Found** anchor questions (63 + 23 = 86 of 165) — the ones needing zero prep. Prepared (25) and Situational (32) come later.

### 3d. Circle-back (the feedback baseline)
Already named in the project's own taxonomy — Retrospective-anchored questions (22 of 165) are "Circle back" in the anchoring table, and this surface is where a lightweight reflection could eventually ride the Cochrane-backed video-feedback mechanism (SMD 0.34, moderate-certainty evidence).

**For v1, keep this surface's job narrow:**
- One tap, end of session: *"Did your child ask a question?"* — the exact child-side metric the positioning memo names (§8) as the thing that would catch a Lu-et-al.-style failure (adult feels good, child disengaged) that adult-side metrics can't see.
- Optional short text note.
- **Not audio capture in v1.**
- Submissions post to the lightweight backend in §2, tagged with the device's session ID, first name, optional email, and age band — this is what makes the weekly digest (§3e) possible.

### 3e. Weekly digest — for the founder, not for families

**Confirmed: v1 builds a data digest that lands with the founder, not an auto-generated report sent to families.** Per family, per week: which questions were asked, their tags, the circle-back taps and notes, age band. The founder reads it and, if he chooses, writes the actual feedback back to a family himself — the way a teacher writes a progress note, using his own judgment rather than the app's.

An app that auto-generates math-specific feedback copy sent directly to families is a real feature, but a bigger and riskier one than "tag, don't diagnose" (§4) allows for right now. **Explicitly not committing v1's capacity to a parent-facing version.**

## 4. Knowledge/skill layer (counting ability) — tag now, don't diagnose yet

**Recommendation for v1: tag, don't score.**
- Tag each bank question with which Gelman & Gallistel counting principle (see `counting-principles-blurbs-takeaways.md`) or CGI problem type it touches, where applicable (Category L "number stories" already maps to this almost directly).
- Do **not** build a diagnostic or skill-assessment feature in v1.
- Any skill-related guidance shown to the adult must follow the same rule as the answer seed: **after** the interaction, framed as observation, never as instruction beforehand.
- The weekly pick's domain mix (§3b) and this tagging plan are the same idea applied twice: protect the non-math remainder deliberately.

## 5. Audience

Small warm test group first (people the founder knows personally), then opening to podcast listeners / friends-of-friends — see §2 for how this shapes the identity/reachability design.

## 6. Explicit non-goals for v1

- No audio/video capture
- No skill diagnostic or scoring
- **No parent-facing auto-generated feedback report** — the weekly digest goes to the founder; a family-facing version is a distinct, larger feature, deliberately not committed to here.
- No Prepared or Situational question delivery (UI for advance-assembly / routine-triggers)
- No daily-engagement or streak mechanic
- No hard-blocking on the onboarding comprehension check
- No re-offering the weekly pick more than once a week
- No real authentication (PINs, magic links, password accounts) — continuity rests on the device's session ID; first name is a label only
- No exact child age or child name captured — age band only
